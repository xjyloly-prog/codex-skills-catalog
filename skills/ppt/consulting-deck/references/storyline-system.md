# Storyline system

## Governing thought

Write one governing thought that answers the audience's central question. Require it
to be:

- specific enough to disagree with;
- supported by the source evidence;
- relevant to a decision;
- short enough to repeat from memory.

Avoid empty claims such as “the industry is changing rapidly” or “AI brings
opportunities and challenges.”

## Select a narrative spine

Choose the simplest structure that fits the job.

### SCQA

Use for a recommendation or change case:

1. Situation — what is stable and agreed.
2. Complication — what changed or creates tension.
3. Question — what the audience must decide.
4. Answer — the governing thought and response.

### What / So what / Now what

Use for research summaries and executive updates:

1. What happened?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What should the audience do?

### Diagnosis / Choice / Mobilization

Use for strategy and transformation:

1. Diagnose the performance gap or opportunity.
2. Compare strategic choices and trade-offs.
3. Define initiatives, owners, milestones, and measures.

### Hypothesis / Evidence / Implication

Use when evidence is incomplete or the deck is part of a working session. Label
hypotheses explicitly and do not present them as facts.

## Build horizontal logic

The sequence of slide titles should read like a short executive memo. Test it without
looking at slide bodies.

Require:

- no repeated conclusion in adjacent slides;
- no unsupported jump between diagnosis and action;
- a clear transition at every section boundary;
- recommendations that answer the diagnosed causes;
- an appendix that holds detail rather than hiding weak logic.

## Build vertical logic

Each slide should contain:

1. an answer-first title;
2. one dominant evidence structure;
3. annotations that explain why the evidence matters;
4. a source or methodology note when required.

Do not place multiple competing charts or frameworks on one slide unless comparison
is the slide's explicit job.

## Write action titles

Use a complete claim with a subject and implication.

| Weak topic title | Strong action title |
| --- | --- |
| AI adoption | AI adoption is broadening, but most organizations remain stuck in pilots |
| Bank profitability | Record bank profits mask a fragile return outlook |
| Customer behavior | Falling loyalty makes the initial consideration set the new battleground |
| Recommendations | Four precision moves can protect returns while funding the next growth curve |

For Chinese decks:

- target roughly 16–32 Chinese characters;
- prefer one line;
- use punctuation only when it clarifies the logic;
- avoid slogans that cannot be proven.

For English decks:

- prefer sentence case;
- target roughly 8–16 words;
- lead with the conclusion, not “Overview of” or “Analysis of.”

## Use page roles

Assign one role to each slide:

- `orient` — establish context or section.
- `assert` — state a decisive insight.
- `prove` — present evidence.
- `compare` — show alternatives or segments.
- `explain` — show a mechanism or system.
- `decide` — frame a choice or priority.
- `mobilize` — define actions, owners, or timing.
- `reference` — provide methodology or supporting detail.

Do not use an `orient` slide where the audience needs a conclusion.

## Executive Summary

Make the Executive Summary independently readable. Include:

- governing thought;
- 3–5 supporting insights;
- 2–4 recommended actions when the deck is decision-oriented;
- key quantified evidence;
- explicit uncertainty where relevant.

Do not summarize slide topics. Summarize conclusions.

## Research-summary recipe

Use this default sequence for a 9–11 slide research deck:

1. Cover
2. Executive Summary
3. Context or inflection point
4. First decisive finding
5. Second decisive finding
6. Segment, maturity, or competitive comparison
7. Mechanism or cause
8. Implications
9. Recommended actions
10. Roadmap or decision agenda
11. Sources and methodology, if needed

Adjust the sequence to the material rather than forcing every slot.

## Strategy-decision recipe

Use this default sequence:

1. Cover
2. Executive Summary
3. Performance gap or opportunity
4. Root causes
5. Strategic choices
6. Evaluation criteria
7. Recommended choice
8. Value case
9. Implementation roadmap
10. Risks and mitigations
11. Decision required

## Content editing rules

- Remove background facts the audience already knows.
- Replace adjectives with evidence.
- Replace long prose with a quantified claim, comparison, or mechanism.
- Preserve nuance in notes or appendix when simplification could mislead.
- Keep visible copy audience-facing; never show internal planning language.
- Translate faithfully when the source language differs from the deck language.
- Do not invent quotations, numbers, company examples, or source locators.
